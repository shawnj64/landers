## ⚠️ Compliance Notes Before Launch (Read First)

Native platforms have specific policies for lending offers. Every line below was written to survive editorial review:

- **No implied guarantees.** No "guaranteed approval," "no credit check," "everyone approved," or "instant cash."
- **No rate or amount claims** in headlines (amounts and APR ranges belong on the lander, disclosed properly — MLA caps at 36% MAPR for covered borrowers).
- **No fake urgency or countdowns.** Native reviewers flag these fast.
- **No sensationalized clickbait** ("This one trick," "Banks hate this") — these get throttled or rejected on Outbrain in the finance category.
- **Quiz framing must be honest.** The click must lead to an actual quiz, not a disguised application form. Headlines below promise "answer a few questions" / "check what you qualify for" — the lander must deliver exactly that.
- **Do not use branch names, unit names, insignia, or official military imagery** in creatives. Implied government/DoD endorsement is a policy violation across both platforms and a legal risk.
- **Geo-target to licensed states only.** Wasted native spend on unlicensed geos is the fastest way to torch a budget on this offer.

## Image Direction (Native Requires Editorial-Looking Creative)

Native creative fails when it looks like an ad. Direct your designer toward:

- A plain phone screen showing a low bank balance and a calendar (no logos, no branding)
- A half-packed moving box in an empty living room, natural light
- A kitchen table with a calculator, a stack of mail, and coffee
- A person in a plain t-shirt at a laptop, shot from behind, slightly out of focus

**Avoid:** uniforms, flags, salutes, stock-photo handshakes, stacks of cash, anything with a logo. Those either get rejected or attract the wrong, non-converting clicker.

## Bridge Copy — First 3 Lines of the Quiz Lander

Native traffic is cold and skeptical. The click was made on curiosity, so the top of the quiz page has to *finish the thought* before it asks for anything. Use this above the first question:

## Quiz Question Framing (Copy Support)

Keep questions low-friction and self-diagnostic. Order matters — easiest first, PII last:

1. Are you active duty, a veteran, or a military family member?
2. Which best describes what you need funds for? *(Vehicle repair / Moving costs / Family emergency / Bills between pay periods / Other)*
3. How soon do you need it? *(This week / Within two weeks / Just planning ahead)*
4. Roughly how long have you been in your current pay grade?
5. Which range best fits what you're looking for?
6. Have you been turned down by a bank or credit union in the past year?

Then the contact step, framed as: **"Last step — where should we send your options?"**

## What I'd Test in Week 4

- **Headline structure:** Question format (#4, #6) vs. situational statement (#1, #3). Native audiences usually split hard one way; find yours.
- **Number specificity:** "6 questions" (#8) vs. "a few questions." Specific numbers typically lift quiz-start rate.
- **Quiz length:** 6 questions vs. 4. Longer quizzes often improve lead quality on lending offers even as start-to-finish rate drops — measure on funded rate, not lead volume.
- **Bridge copy length:** The three-paragraph version above vs. a single sentence. Native traffic sometimes needs the warm-up; sometimes it just wants the quiz.

**Do not scale any of this until trailing EPC is confirmed above $0.80 and the network's compliance sheet is on file.** Native spend moves fast and this vertical is watched closely.
