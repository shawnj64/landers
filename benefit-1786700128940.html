She Tested a New Career for $50 Before Quitting Her Job — Here's What Her Life Looks Like Now

No bootcamp debt. No CS degree. Just a low-risk way to find out if a tech career was actually possible for her — before betting everything on it.

By Marissa Colton  •  Contributor  •  Updated This Week

If you've ever sat at your desk doing the math — how many more years until you can afford to leave — this story is going to feel uncomfortably familiar.

That was Dana, 31, a shift supervisor at a regional retail chain, six months ago. Not miserable, exactly. Just stuck. Same schedule, same ceiling, watching people two rungs above her make barely more money for a lot more stress. She'd think about "doing something different" the way most people think about going to the gym — a nice idea for a future version of herself that never quite arrived.

⸻

The Before: Stuck, Tired, and Talked Herself Out of It Twice

Dana had tried to make a change before. Twice, actually. The first time, she opened a free coding tutorial site, made it through two lessons, and closed the tab when work got busy. It never got reopened.

The second time was more serious — she looked into a coding bootcamp. Fifteen thousand dollars. Full-time for three months. She didn't have the savings to cover rent without an income, let alone tuition. She closed that browser tab too, but this one stung more, because for a second she'd let herself believe it was possible.

"I wasn't lazy," she told me. "I just didn't trust that it would actually go anywhere. Every option was either too vague to commit to, or too expensive to risk being wrong about."

"I didn't need someone to tell me I *could* learn to code. I needed a way to find out for fifty dollars instead of finding out for fifteen thousand."

The Turning Point: A Different Kind of Test

What changed wasn't motivation — Dana says she never lacked that. What changed was finding a way to test the entire idea of a tech career on a small, low-stakes scale before making any real commitment.

A friend who'd made a similar switch pointed her toward a structured, self-paced online learning platform — the kind built around actual guided tracks and real projects, not just a pile of disconnected videos. The starting cost was close to what she'd spend on takeout in a week, not a year of savings. She figured she had nothing to lose by trying it for one month.

That one month became the test that changed everything.
